Getting here & chalet access

Access to Zermatt: train & parking in Täsch

Zermatt village is car-free, so we recommend booking a Parking & Train ticket at the Matterhorn Terminal in Täsch. From there a shuttle train takes you to Zermatt in 12 minutes.

  • Matterhorn Terminal Täsch — information (zermatt.swiss)
  • Book your parking directly (Matterhorn Gotthard Bahn)
  • If you are not certain about the duration of your stay, just book the first days: if you stay longer the price is adjusted pro rata, but you will need to pay at the Täsch train station counter when you return to the parking.
  • Prefer the 1st floor, which spares you the need to take a lift.
  • You will need a CHF 5 coin (deposit) to get a luggage trolley.

Access to the chalet: taxis

Once at the Zermatt train station, electric taxis wait for clients outside. We recommend booking a taxi with the TaxiGo Zermatt app — the chalet's address (Fortuna, Wiestistrasse 200) is already in their system, and you get a CHF 3 discount when ordering online.

In case of fire

Smoking is strictly forbidden everywhere in the apartment and the chalet, because of the entirely wooden structure of the building.

There are two smoke sensors in the apartment; should a fire develop, they will beep. To put out a fire you will find:

  • a fire blanket in the apartment, on the wall next to the entrance door (red box)
  • a fire extinguisher in the staircase

In case of fire: leave the apartment immediately, call 118, and inform the people living upstairs and downstairs.

Zermatt in winter

Skiing — the 3 areas & how to get there

Zermatt has three main ski areas which are all connected, so wherever you start you can ski the whole resort without having to come back down into the village. Official piste map (PDF)

1. Rothorn – Sunnegga Paradise

This is the area closest to Chalet Fortuna. It's a beautiful and sunny area, with mostly red slopes with incredible views and scenery. You find the best mountain restaurants in the Findeln area. There is a big beginners' park at Leisee, just off the Sunnegga metro.

There are also some yellow slopes near Kumme and you can easily reach the yellow runs around Rote Nase: please note that yellow slopes are only secured and NOT prepared, so these are only for expert skiers!

How to get there: the easiest from the chalet is to walk down Wiestistrasse until after Hotel Christiania, where you will find a staircase on your right: walk down the stairs and you'll be right at the departure of the Sunnegga mountain metro (a funicular inside a mountain tunnel). You can buy your ski pass directly there.

2. Gornergrat

This area is more in the shade, so depending on weather conditions runs can be more icy — but snow can also be of better quality than in the rest of the resort.

You can either take the Gornergrat Bahn up, which leaves opposite the main station (trains run only every 20 minutes), or connect through Sunnegga Paradise. From Chalet Fortuna it is definitely better to go up to Sunnegga with the mountain metro and then connect through either Blauherd – Gant – Hohtälli (intermediate or good skiers) or Sunnegga – Findeln – Breitboden (less experienced skiers).

The area around the Gornergrat Bahn, especially Riffelberg, is quite easy, with a beginners' park and lots of blue runs. However, the area further up, between Hohtälli and Rote Nase, offers the most difficult skiing in Zermatt (open from January). Here you will find most of Zermatt's yellow runs: freeride pistes that are only secured, NOT prepared — only for expert skiers with good nerves who are not afraid of heights. You may find anything from powder snow to huge icy bumps, or a mix of both. You may also go up with a guide if you are not sure you can make it down safely.

3. Matterhorn Glacier Paradise

This is the biggest and highest ski area in Zermatt (and in Europe). Skiing on the glacier around Klein Matterhorn is possible all year round. If you want to ski down to Cervinia or Valtournenche on the Italian side, you need the international ski pass.

Altitude: you'll go up as high as 3,800 m. If you have never been at that altitude it can be quite a shock for your body — trouble breathing, nausea or strong headaches. Don't go there on the first day of your stay; allow time to adjust, and go up gradually: ski a bit around Schwarzsee before venturing further up towards Testa Grigia. If you experience a problem, go down immediately — below 2,000 m you will feel fine again.

The runs are varied: up on the glacier the slopes are very gentle and thus good for beginners, or to finish a tough day. Around Schwarzsee more experienced skiers will find more interesting and difficult runs. Note that this area is usually the most crowded: many skiers come up from Italy, and from the Swiss side many want to ski close to the Matterhorn with its astounding views. Runs on the Italian side are usually crowded and not that interesting, but it's certainly worth trying them once so you can make up your own mind.

How to get there: take the ski bus. Walk down Wiestistrasse, take the first right (Sunneggastrasse) and cross the bridge over the river: the bus stop is right there on your left (opposite Hotel Ginabelle). The bus is free with a ski pass and drops you at the cable car. At the end of the day, the same bus takes you back home.

Ski rental, ski schools and guides

Ski rental. The most practical is to rent at the departure of the Sunnegga metro or at Matterhorn Glacier Paradise: there are shops and rental places around both departure points.

Ski schools. Zermatt offers a host of ski schools with group and private lessons for children and adults. The biggest ones:

  • Ski- & Snowboardschule Zermatters (also children's courses) — Snow & Alpine Center, Bahnhofstrasse 58 · 027 966 24 66
  • Stoked – The SkiSchool & Swiss Snowboard School (also children's courses) — Hofmattstrasse 7 · 027 967 70 20
  • Many other offers: zermatt.ch/skischule

Beginners' park. Wolli's park for beginners by the frozen Leisee at Sunnegga is perfect for practising: take the Sunnegga metro and then the self-service Leisee shuttle. The sunny, sheltered area has three magic carpets, two rope tows and a range of attractions. Tickets are much cheaper if you only use the beginners' area (includes the Sunnegga metro, Leisee and the Eisfluh blue run), so do specify that when buying your ticket.

Off-piste / heli-skiing. If you want to go off-piste, never go on your own: the Zermatt area is among the most beautiful off-piste terrain in Europe, but also very dangerous, as much of it is on glaciers in extreme alpine conditions, and avalanches are very common. Always take an experienced mountain guide. The one-stop shop for guides and heli-skiing is the Alpin Center Zermatt, Bahnhofstrasse 58 · 027 966 24 60.

Zermatt in summer

Zermatt offers an incredible quantity of activities in the summer; please always drop by the Tourism office next to the station to see the detailed weekly programme: zermatt.swiss/activities

Hiking

With more than 400 km of marked hiking and walking trails, Zermatt, Täsch and Randa offer a wide range of options. The mountain railways make it possible to effortlessly reach altitudes of up to 3,000 m. You can obtain a free hiking map at the Tourism office, where you can also buy guidebooks and more detailed maps.

A few suggestions for easy walks in the 2-hour range, also suitable with children:

Sunnegga – Findeln

From Fortuna, walk down to the Sunnegga departure and take the mountain metro up. From Sunnegga, walk down to Leisee and then Findeln, where you'll find incredible mountain restaurants (Chez Vrony) with sunny terraces facing the Matterhorn. Then walk down towards Zermatt but take the "Höhenweg" towards Ried instead of going down to Winkelmatten: you keep walking at this higher level with the village underneath and come down later, closer to the Wiesti quarter where Fortuna lies.

Ried

When leaving Fortuna, take a right and walk in the opposite direction from the Matterhorn, following the signs for Ried. It's quite a steep climb at the beginning, but once you've reached the "Höhenweg" it's more even. You can reach the Ried restaurant or continue further towards Othmar's Hütte and Tufteren.

Blatten and the Gorner Gorge

Walk towards the Matterhorn until the end of the village. Keep to the right of the river and follow the signs up to Blatten, a couple of old wooden barns and the delicious Blatten restaurant; Zum See (another famous restaurant) is easily reached from here. In Blatten, kids can enjoy the Ricola herb garden and the swings in front of the restaurant. To come back down, cross through the Gorner Gorge ("Gornerschlucht"), a breathtaking gorge over the river with narrow steps — not a good idea if you are afraid of heights, with very small children, or after heavy rain, as it gets extremely slippery. A small visitor fee is payable at the gorge.

Glacier garden

From Furi (cable car or on foot), walk up following the signs for "Gletschergarten". The Gletschergarten Dossen is a witness of the last Ice Age: the retreat of the Gornergletscher, the second-largest glacier in the Alps, has exposed potholes created by meltwater flowing down to the glacier bed through crevasses. A very interesting hike back into the Ice Age with lots of geology explanations — great for school children and adults alike.

5-lakes hike / Grünsee

Take the Sunnegga metro up to Blauherd and walk the 5-lakes trail, a very nice walk in beautiful scenery ending at Sunnegga (about 4 hours). Ask for a detailed map at the Tourism office. A faster but also beautiful alternative: take the Gornergrat train up to Riffelalp, walk to Grünsee and back.

Mountaineering and climbing

Zermatt has its roots in alpine sport and mountaineering. 65 licensed mountain guides know all about the mountain world around the Matterhorn, with its 38 four-thousand-metre peaks. People with a good level of fitness — and the appropriate respect for the mountain — can take part in this extreme sport. For mountain guides and excursions, contact the Alpin Center on Bahnhofstrasse (on your right when walking up). Do not go on these tours on your own, especially if this is your first time in Zermatt.

Summer skiing

Enjoy 20 km of pistes on Zermatt's Theodul glacier, carving alongside national ski teams from all over Europe, with the Matterhorn always in sight. Departure is at Matterhorn Glacier Paradise in Winkelmatten, at the other end of the village.

Mountain biking

Over gravel and stones, through larch and Swiss pine forests, on single trails — all with a magnificent mountain backdrop. Six routes with a total of 100 km and different difficulty levels, covering on average 1,000 m of altitude. Try the downhill run and 4cross from Sunnegga. Rent mountain bikes at Bayard Sport or Matterhorn Sport (both on Bahnhofstrasse) or Julen Sport (close to the Migros). Bike maps and further information at the Tourism office.

The peaks

Whatever you do and wherever you go, do not miss the famous peaks, especially Gornergrat. Also very nice: Klein Matterhorn and its Glacier Palace. A nice, less expensive ride is up to Schwarzsee, with an amazing view of the Matterhorn and Monte Rosa at a lower altitude (2,500 m).

Forest Fun Park

A rope adventure park in the forest — great fun for children and adults. Details and opening times at the Tourism office.

Children's programme "Wolli"

Zermatt offers the free Wolli-Pass and activities for children every week. Please contact the Tourism office for the detailed programme.

Other activities

You may also play tennis at the Sport- und Freizeitarena Zermatt, on your way back when walking home from the Migros; swimming is also possible, and playgrounds for children can be found in the same area. For dirt scooters and kick bikes, paragliding, fly fishing and river rafting, please contact Zermatt Tourism.

Zermatt — what to do

Of course you will be busy skiing or hiking. But Zermatt has so much else to offer that it would be a shame to miss out on the whole experience.

Our must-do's and must-see's

In such a magical destination everything is great, but here is our personal list:

  1. The peaks: Gornergrat and Klein Matterhorn, to take in the amazing beauty of the alpine scenery.
  2. The history: the Matterhorn Museum next to the church is really worth a visit for children and adults — don't miss it!
  3. The old village centre, Hinterdorf, with its 16th-century wooden barns; see it once by day and once by night, it's unique!
  4. The best fondue: at Whymperstube, on Bahnhofstrasse on your right when walking up.
  5. The cosiest bar: Elsie's Bar opposite the church, for a glass of wine or a delicious hot chocolate.
  6. Lunch at 3,500 m at Testa Grigia, a tiny restaurant on the Italian side of the glacier: the starter with chestnuts and bacon is incredible.
  7. The best terrace: Chez Vrony in Findeln — the best slope restaurant, with a sunny terrace, deckchairs and a stunning view of the Matterhorn. You won't want to leave…
  8. Live music on the slope at Fluhalp, with wonderful food, a great sunny terrace and an amazing view; about halfway down the red run from Rothorn, following a sign on your left.
  9. The best Rösti: at Blattenwirt in Blatten, on the last slope from Furi to Zermatt (only when there is enough snow, obviously).
  10. Après-ski: several great places along the descent from Sunnegga, starting with the lounge up on Blauherd, followed by various champagne bars until Hotel Cervo at the end of the slope — a fantastic spot to end the day (and its lift takes you down to Sunnegga).
  11. The Vernissage in the Backstage Hotel close to the Migros: a unique experience — a great bar, ciné-dinner and more, from Zermatt's great artist Heinz Julen.
  12. A last drink on your way home or a great après-ski party at Snowboat, next to the river almost under the Gornergrat bridge: nice service, cool drinks and snacks, cool design; also live music and club nights.
  13. Only for expert skiers and in good weather: go up to Rote Nase (open from January) and, before venturing down what can be a great powder descent or a horrible ice-bump run (yellow slopes are only secured, not prepared!), take a moment, sit down in the snow and look at the blue Monte Rosa glacier in front of you: a true glimpse into eternity…

Our favourite restaurants

You will eat well almost everywhere in Zermatt, especially in the mountain restaurants, which frankly rank among the best we have ever experienced on the pistes.

Restaurants in Zermatt fill up very quickly — make a reservation!

Traditional food

  • Whymperstube — Bahnhofstrasse, on the right when walking up, next to the Monte Rosa Hotel.
  • Chez Heini — just opposite Fortuna. Speciality: lamb. 13 points Gault Millau, a very "Swiss" place, and the owner often gives a concert of his own music. Make a reservation!
  • Walliserhof — also on Bahnhofstrasse, good Swiss food.
  • Sonnmatten — 13 points Gault Millau, in Winkelmatten. Good traditional cuisine with an Austrian touch, very kid-friendly, fantastic cakes in the afternoon. Quite a walk from home (25 min) to digest.
  • Zermama — 5 minutes from home, just below the Gorner bridge on the right: a nice small restaurant with great salads and easy food. Fantastic breakfast!
  • Stephanie's Crêperie — take-away on Bahnhofstrasse just after the Alpin Center; delicious!

Gourmet restaurants

On the mountain: Chez Vrony (Findeln), Findlerhof (Findeln), Paradies (Findeln), Zum See (along the descent from the Klein Matterhorn area).

In the village: Hotel Cervo (also great for après-ski), After Seven at Hotel Backstage above the Vernissage, The Omnia, Zermatterhof, Mont Cervin Palace.

Asian: China Garden, Japanese restaurant Myoko.

Après-ski drinks

Here is a list of the 8 best après-ski bars in Zermatt.

Hennustall is the classic après-ski on the Furi–Zermatt home run: you basically ski into it. Super lively from about 15:30 to 18:30 — DJ, dancing on benches, lots of shots.

The Champagne bar is on the way back to Sunnegga, and there is the very lively stop at the Cervo Hotel (video). You can also enjoy a relaxing hot chocolate on the Hotel Riffelalp terrace with a lovely view of the Matterhorn.

Our preferred spots at night

  • Hotel Post — the one place you cannot miss at night, in the middle of Bahnhofstrasse on your left when walking up. Several clubs and restaurants in one location, from live funk or blues to house sounds: something for almost every taste, in a very nice setting.
  • Hotel Backstage & Vernissage — on Hofmattstrasse close to the Migros: the most original cinema you will ever see, but also a bar where you can eat, hang out or sip a great cocktail, and an art gallery.
  • Snowboat — on your way home, next to the Gornergrat railway bridge on the river; also club nights and après-ski parties.
  • Hexenbar on Bahnhofstrasse — nice drinks in a cosy atmosphere; likewise Elsie's Bar opposite the church, which is very intimate.
  • Papperla Pub — for a real disco night: at the church walk down towards the river, cross the bridge and you'll find it on your left.

Children

Spa and massages

Hotel Ginabelle (2 minutes' walk, down by the river) offers various spa packages, massages and offers for couples, which can be booked even the day before. Hotel Mirabeau (5 minutes' walk) also offers a large spa programme for external guests.
